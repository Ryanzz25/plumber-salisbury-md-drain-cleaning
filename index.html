Drain Cleaning in Salisbury, MD: A Clogged Drain Checklist

A clogged drain starts small and gets worse. Work through these questions first and you will know whether you have a single clog, a main line problem or a septic issue.

Is it one fixture or several?

A single slow sink or shower is usually a clog in its own trap or branch line. When more than one fixture backs up at once, the main line is the likely problem, and it often shows first in the lowest drain in the house.

Does a toilet gurgle when a sink drains?

Gurgling from a toilet while a nearby sink or tub drains is a sign the blockage sits deeper in the line, beyond what a plunger can reach.

Which drain is it?

A kitchen sink clogged with grease needs the line cleared beyond the P-trap. A shower drain clogged with hair is usually close to the drain itself. Grease down the kitchen sink is the most common cause, followed by wipes in toilets and hair in showers.

Are you on city sewer or septic?

Roughly half of Wicomico County homes drain to public sewer and half to a septic tank. On septic, several slow drains can point to a full tank or a saturated drainfield, especially from late fall to early spring when the water table is highest.

Have you tried chemical cleaner?

Chemical drain openers rarely reach the real blockage, can damage older pipe, and are hard on the bacteria a septic tank depends on. Mechanical cleaning is the safer fix.

Snake or hydro jetting?

A drain snake or auger breaks through a single blockage. Hydro jetting scours the whole pipe wall with high-pressure water, which suits heavy grease and clogs that keep coming back.

Does the same drain keep backing up?

A camera through the cleanout shows root intrusion, sagging pipe or broken joints. About a quarter of Salisbury homes were built before 1960, so a camera also shows the condition of older drain pipe before anyone decides how hard to clean it.
